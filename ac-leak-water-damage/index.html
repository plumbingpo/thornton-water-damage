Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Thornton

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. With an air handler in the attic or a hallway closet, the overflow goes straight into the ceiling, wall or floor around it.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ACSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Leave the system off until it is fixed.

Coverage

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Timing is usually what decides whether an AC leak is covered. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • A missing or failed float switch that should have shut the system off.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • Missing insulation on cold lines and ductwork, so condensation forms and falls.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Baseboards and Door Trim

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Mold Near the Return

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Attic Insulation

Batts or blown-in material next to the pan absorb the leak and press that moisture onto the ceiling. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Nearby Flooring

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.
