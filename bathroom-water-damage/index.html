Bathroom Water Damage in Thornton

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. When caulk cracks, grout wears thin or a toilet seal fails, a little water slips past with every shower and every flush. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Thornton, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

Signs to Look For

  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Wiring and light fixtures may lie along that route.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Slow Vanity Drips

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.
