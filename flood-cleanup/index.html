A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. Floodwater is almost always rated Category 2 (gray water) or Category 3 (black water), so it carries bacteria, contaminants and possibly hazardous material that can make people seriously ill. For flood emergencies anywhere in Thornton, Thornton Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. After that, IICRC-certified technicians on our team measure how contaminated the area is, then sanitize it with antimicrobial products, HEPA-filtered air cleaning and targeted disinfection of all surfaces the water reached. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. From start to finish we document each step for your insurance claim, with the moisture maps, equipment logs, photos and scope-of-work reports adjusters ask for.

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Use waterproof bins and raised shelving for anything valuable kept at ground level or below
  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Wherever flooding is likely, get appliances and electrical equipment up above floor level
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors

Why Hire a Professional for Flood Cleanup?

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. Protecting your family from E. coli, hepatitis and similar waterborne illness takes proper containment, correct use of protective equipment (PPE) and proven sanitizing methods, all things professional teams train for.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Water flooding out of a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine
  • A water line on walls or baseboards showing how high it rose
  • A sump pump that failed during heavy rain and let the basement flood
  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls
